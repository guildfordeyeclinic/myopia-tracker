Absolute natural lens power (modified Bennett)

Hernandez et al. modification of Bennett’s method — crystalline lens power from cycloplegic refraction, ACD, LT, AL, and mean keratometry.

Right eye (OD)

Left eye (OS)

Formula: Hernandez VM, et al. Calculation of crystalline lens power using a modification of the Bennett method. Biomed Opt Express. 2015;6(11):4501–4515. Uses thin-lens position b·LT with conjugate-ratio–dependent b (Eqs. 16–18). Posterior corneal radius estimated as 0.81 × anterior if not measured. CCT defaults to 0.55 mm.

Enter ACD from epithelium to anterior lens (usual biometer ACD). Prefer cycloplegic SE. Educational estimate only — theoretical accuracy ≈ ±0.5 D for relaxed eyes (−10 to +10 D).

Interpretation guide: typical natural lens power is about +20 to +24 D. If power is ≤ +16 D, residual crystalline-lens buffering is considered gone.

Sources & methods

  • Clinical framing: Poon N. Axial Length: An Essential for Myopia Management. BC Doctors of Optometry.
  • European curves: Tideman JWL, et al. Axial length growth and the risk of developing myopia in European children. Acta Ophthalmol. 2018;96(3):301-309. (P25/P50/P75 anchors; intermediate ages interpolated; outer percentiles estimated.)
  • East Asian curves: Sanz Diez P, et al. LMS parameters, percentile, and Z-score growth curves for axial length in Chinese schoolchildren in Wuhan. Sci Rep. 2022;12:4850. Ages 6–15 from published LMS tables; 16–18 lightly extended for display.
  • Also related: Diez et al. 2019 growth curves; He et al. 2023 Chinese AL/AL-CR percentiles; IMI clinical management guidelines.
  • Crystalline lens power: Hernandez VM, et al. Calculation of crystalline lens power using a modification of the Bennett method. Biomed Opt Express. 2015;6(11):4501–4515 (PMC4646557). Based on Bennett AG (1988); thin-lens position and b coefficient per Eqs. 16–18.
